Tools

QGIS plugins for drainage and terrain work. Each card links to the plugin's folder on GitHub, with its README, install steps and source code, and to its zip file for installing in QGIS. Once installed, the drainage tools are grouped under Vector → Tile Drainage and Raster → Tile Drainage in QGIS.

Plugin · v0.6.0

Illini Main Tile Locator

Finds the best tile main from a clicked outlet, a DEM and a field boundary, for laterals parallel or perpendicular to the contours. Scores dozens of candidate mains by how much of the field their laterals can drain to, buries them with Illini Drainage Tools routine J, and returns the chosen main and four runners-up, labelled by rank. Can also add submains until the field is covered. Maximum lateral length defaults to the capacity of a 4-inch tile on a 0.1% slope. A curved main can be smoothed or turned into straight sections of a minimum length, and the chosen main is also written in straight sections as a layer that opens in Tile Profile.

Plugin · v0.23.0

Illini Subsurface Drain Layout

Lays out, buries and sizes a tile drainage network in one run, from a DEM, field boundary, outlet and spacing, using the burying and pipe-sizing schemes of Illini Drainage Tools routines J and K. Laterals can be straight (following the terrain or running north-south, east-west or diagonally), follow the contours, or follow the contours in the 8 compass directions (8-point). The network stores tile and ground elevations at every vertex for the Tile Profile plugin. An optional Tile elevation raster highlights where the tile is shallower than the minimum cover or deeper than the maximum depth. No tile bends more sharply than 120 degrees, and the tool remembers its settings between runs. Optional no-tile zones (ponds, waterways, building sites) keep laterals out, with the option of letting mains cross them. Tile can be buried with Routine J, as shallow as the minimum cover and grade allow, with the fewest grade breaks, or at least cost (grade breaks balanced against distance from the target depth), with an optional limit on the change of grade at a break and an option to hold the outlet at a fixed invert. Pipes are sized with one drainage coefficient (0.375 inch/day by default), ready for the Tile Size Calculator. A second tool imports LANDRAIN network files as Illini networks.

Plugin · v0.5.0

Illini Subirrigation Layout

Lays out a tile network for subirrigation and controlled drainage in one run: splits the field into water management zones by elevation (or from drawn zone polygons), lays laterals within each zone, places a control structure with its board elevation for each zone, and buries and sizes the network with the Illini Subsurface Drain Layout engine. A Level within zones burying method keeps the tile of each zone near level and below the zone's board, so all of it can take water in from the structure. Reports the share of each zone the managed water table controls and the share of tile below the board. Optional no-tile zones keep tile out of ponds, waterways and building sites.

Plugin · v0.8.0

Tile Profile

Select a lateral or main and a docked panel draws its profile: the ground, the target and absolute tile-depth lines, and the tile with its grade breaks, junctions and section slopes. Flat and reverse sections are flagged. Check network maps where the tile is too shallow or too deep and lists the profiles to check. The whole network, single points, or the slope above a point can be adjusted, and break points can be added to a line so part of a section takes a different slope; changes are previewed and applied to the layer, with BURY_CHECK rechecked.

Plugin · v0.2.1

Tile Size Calculator

Resizes the pipes of an Illini Subsurface Drain Layout network in a docked panel. The drainage coefficient can be one value for every line, one per Strahler order, or set pipe by pipe by clicking pipes on the map, and the pipe type can be set for the whole network or section by section along a main. The new length of each pipe size shows at once, and the sizes are written to the layer with Routine K of the Illini Drainage Tools. A Bill of materials tab lists pipe by size, tees, wyes, reducers, elbows, end caps, outlet pipe, rodent guard, structures and installation, prices them from an editable price table, totals them by NRCS practice for EQIP, and makes a ready-to-print quote layout or a custom layout from a template. Water control structures can be counted from a Control structures layer on the map.

Plugin · v0.1.0

Drainage intensity

Maps how fast a designed tile network can drain each part of a field (in/day or mm/day), by the Hooghoudt equation with the water table between the drains or the Kirkham equation for a ponded surface, as in DRAINMOD. Reads the Drainage Network from the Illini layout and design tools: each drain's depth comes from its tile and ground elevations, and the local spacing from the layout itself (out to the drainage divide, or the field edge), so the strips between laterals, the wedges at junctions and the poorly drained ground past lateral ends all show. Soil from the SSURGO or SoilGrids drainage soil maps, worked out at each drain's own depth, or uniform values. Drawn in classes against a target drainage coefficient, with the field's mean and median and the share below the target.

Plugin · v0.1.0

Surface and blind inlets

Places a surface inlet (riser with an orifice plate) or a blind inlet (gravel trench under a sand filter) at the low point of each depression, such as those from Depressions and Laylines, and connects it to the nearest main or submain of a Drainage Network from the Illini tools. From the DEM it finds each pond's level, depth and volume and its direct drainage area; it sets the design flow from the pond's drawdown time or a surface-water coefficient, sizes the orifice or filter and the lead pipe (grade from the tile elevation and minimum cover, size by Manning's equation), and follows the network down to the outlet to check every pipe's capacity for the added flow, with the size it would need. Outputs inlets, lead pipes with tile and ground elevations, and a tile check drawn green and red.

Plugin · v3.0.1

Illini Drainage System Design

Designs a complete tile drainage system in one run, from drawn tile lines to buried depths and pipe sizes, combining Illini Drainage Tools routines E to K. Tile can be buried with Routine J, as shallow as the minimum cover allows, with the fewest grade breaks, or at least cost, optionally from a fixed outlet invert. The output is the same drainage network as Illini Subsurface Drain Layout's, ready for Tile Profile and the Tile Size Calculator, so it can also be used to re-bury a modified surface layout from Illini Subsurface Drain Layout.

Plugin · v2.6.0

Drainage Outlet

Traces the natural downhill path from a drainage system outlet point, or from a field boundary, to the nearest stream inside its StreamStats watershed. For a field, finds where most of its runoff leaves the boundary. Fills depressions in the DEM, follows the steepest-descent flow path, and reports the path length, drop, slope and the stream it reaches. Uses NHD flowlines or channels derived from the DEM. If you provide a district drain or district tile line, it also traces a path from each outlet to it, following natural flow where it gets there and otherwise routing along the lowest ground, and reports the climb and the deepest cut needed. Works in feet or metres, can download and keep a USGS 3DEP DEM, and runs in the background.

Plugin · v0.1.0

Tile Outlet Submergence

How much a rising water level in the outlet ditch reduces the flow from a tile drainage system, for a network from Illini Subsurface Drain Layout and the DEM. Steps the hydraulic grade line up the network from the ditch level with Manning's equation and reduces the drainage rate wherever water stands in the drains, with Hooghoudt's equation, iterated to a steady state. Writes a rating curve of outflow and affected area against the ditch level (also as a CSV file), maps of the drainage rate and of the HGL rise in every pipe at each level, and an HTML report. A Ditch level explorer panel, also in the report, steps the map up and down through the ditch levels with the outflow, a flap-gate warning, the main's profile and the values at a clicked point.

Plugin · v0.5.1

Yield Compare

Compares the yields of two or more fields (up to eight), or years or treatments, from yield-monitor point layers, and checks whether yield drops midway between tile drains. Gives the difference in mean yield with a 95% confidence interval and tests whether the fields have equal means, medians, variances and histograms, with every test allowing for spatial autocorrelation and harvest-pass effects, so thousands of points do not make every difference look significant. With three or more fields, adds pairwise comparisons adjusted for the number of pairs. With a tile network from Illini Subsurface Drain Layout, compares yield over the tile with yield midway between laterals and beyond the reach of the tile, a field check of the drain spacing, and tests whether the midway loss differs between fields. Also regresses yield on another column, gives robust statistics and exceedance values, and colors both layers by yield. Comes with sample data to try it on: two synthetic fields with known answers, and four real drained strips with their tile network, location masked. A QGIS port of the University of Illinois Yield Statistics program.

County Simulations

Runs DRAINMOD for a county and soil over a range of drain depths and spacings and charts corn and soybean relative yield and the water balance against drain spacing, to find the design that maximizes yield under current and projected climate, with or without drainage water management. The site, weather and soil come online: observed weather from Daymet, projected weather from a NASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 climate model and emissions scenario (1996–2025, 2040–2069 or 2070–2099), and soil from the Illinois soil database or SSURGO; or use .RAI and .TEM files you already have. Needs DRAINMOD (free from NC State).

Excel workbook · v1.4

DRAINMOD Calibration

Calibrates a DRAINMOD project to measured daily drain flow, water table depth or both by maximizing the Kling-Gupta efficiency, with Dynamically Dimensioned Search, several runs side by side and a final polish. A sensitivity analysis (Morris elementary effects) first ranks the parameters, from soil layer Ksat, surface storage and seepage to drain depth, spacing and depth to the impermeable layer. Writes the calibrated project next to the original, which is never changed.

Download zip file
Excel workbook · v2

DRAINMOD Drainage Design

Runs a DRAINMOD project for every combination of drain depth, drain spacing and weather set, current and future, several runs side by side, and charts relative yield (overall, excess water, drought, planting delay) against drain spacing for each crop, to pick a design that holds up in a changing climate. For a chosen drain depth and spacing, its POE analysis gives the probability of exceedance of daily drain flow, the design drain flow and the monthly design flows, with current and future weather on the same charts. Comes with DDESIGN, an example Drummer soil project with Champaign weather for 1980–2010 and 2040–2099; add DRAINMOD (free from NC State) to run it.

Economic Analysis
